Using CryptoTrace

    
Start an investigation
Enter a wallet, choose the correct network, and start with one hop. A hop is one step through the observed transaction relationships. Saved investigations reopen the original retrieved snapshot.

    
Read the graph
Nodes are wallets; Bitcoin also has diamond-shaped transaction nodes. Arrows show observed transfer direction, not proven movement of the same stolen funds. Purple identifies the reported wallet, regardless of its score.
Hover for the address, score, reasons and copy button. Click to pin the card, or use the node selector with a keyboard. Focus connections temporarily hides unrelated records; Show all nodes restores them. Reports always include the entire retrieved snapshot.
Auto uses repulsion up to 150 nodes and spaced hops above that. Fit graph resets the view; zoom and panning are bounded. Crossing edges can still occur in a dense graph.

    
Understand scores and missing transactions
Risk scores support investigator review and do not establish criminal conduct. Every trace records provider coverage, retrieval time and truncation. A disconnected origin is not a clean-wallet finding.
Partial means a limit or retrieval problem occurred. Unavailable means the origin requests failed. No transfers found means no qualifying records were retrieved, not that there has never been activity. Observed means supported provider windows were exhausted within the configured scope.

    
Reports and NCRP
The dossier is an investigator-review draft, not an NCRP approval or an electronic-evidence certificate. Preserve source records and consult the receiving agency about its report template, review and certification process. The snapshot digest does not digitally sign the PDF.
